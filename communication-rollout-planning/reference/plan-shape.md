# Plan shape

```markdown
# Communication plan: <topic>

**Outcome:** <awareness | alignment | decision | execution>
**Audience:** <segment> — <size band>, <composition>, <valence>
**Timeline:** T = <date or event>
**Sensitivity:** <low | medium | high>
**Subjects of the content:** <names, or "none">

## At a glance

| # | Timing | Move | Channel | Audience | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | T-<n>d | <move> | <specific channel> | <segment> | <one line> |

## Move by move

### 1. <move> — <channel> (<timing>)

**Why this move:** <1–2 sentences tied to the intake, not to general principle>

**Draft copy:**

> <the full message, agenda, or script. Placeholders as [link], [date] —
> never invented>

## Why this shape

<2–4 sentences: the ordering logic, which move carries the most weight, and
which profile conventions are being honored.>

## Risks

- <flags that apply: too-lean for the relationship, oversized venue, exclusion,
  register mismatch, duplicate broadcast, misleading summary>
- <profile constraints under strain: notice periods, restricted channels>
- <what this plan does not solve>
```

The header carries the subjects of the content on its own line because that is
the field that reorders the whole plan, and the one a sender omits when they are
thinking about the broadcast.

Draft copy carries placeholders rather than plausible values. A date or a link
invented to complete a draft ships, because everything around it is right.
