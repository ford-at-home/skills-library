# Writing tenets so they survive review

Tenets are written when an initiative is authorized, not when it is reviewed. Add a
`## Tenets` section to the authorizing document rather than creating a separate
artifact nobody will find. Three to five, each with:

- **Statement.** "If we are wrong about `<specific claim about the customer, the
  market, or the technology>`, we should stop investing in this."
- **What evidence would falsify it**, and where that evidence would show up.
- **What this tenet is NOT** — one sentence explicitly ruling out adjacent claims.
  This is the field people skip and the one that does the most work; without it the
  tenet gets narrowed at review time to whatever evidence exists, and then passes
  truthfully while meaning nothing.
- **Owner** who watches external evidence against it, and who is *not* the
  initiative's owner. Owners marking their own tenets fail at the rate you'd expect.

A tenet is disqualified if it is a metric rather than a belief, if nothing
observable could falsify it, if it is the original hypothesis restated, or if it was
added because someone asked whether there should be a tenet about X. Tenets are
load-bearing, not exhaustive.
