# Preparing the rubric

A bare principle name is not reviewable. "Ownership" tells a reviewer nothing
about what to look for. Before scoring, convert each supplied principle into
three lines:

1. **What it means for the purpose of reading a written artifact** — not what it
   means as a value. "Ownership" becomes "every commitment, risk, and follow-up
   names an individual who owns it."
2. **What strong evidence looks like** — concrete, findable in text.
3. **What weak evidence looks like** — the near-miss version that passes a casual
   read. This is the more useful of the two: a reviewer told only what to reward
   rewards surface form.

Do this for every principle before scoring any of them, and show the rubric in
the output. A rubric written during scoring gets bent toward whatever the
document happens to contain.
