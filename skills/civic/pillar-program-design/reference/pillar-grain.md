# How many pillars, and how coarse each one should be

## Count

Pillars = `min(committee_capacity, 8)`, with 5–8 the working range. Statements =
two per pillar.

Capacity is a hard cap, never exceeded: an under-staffed pillar produces vague
statements, no ally, and participants who spend the event guessing at intent.
Below five the program gets thin but still works. Below three, stop and say so
rather than shrinking the count — a two-pillar event is a single-track event, and
calling it a pillar program tells participants to expect a choice they will not
get.

The count is bounded by capacity rather than by the plan's shape. A plan with
fourteen named priorities does not get fourteen pillars; priorities merge, split,
or wait for next cycle.

## Grain

Seven pillars from a mid-size US city program, as an illustration of the grain to
aim for — broad enough to hold two unrelated statements, narrow enough that a
builder knows whether it is for them:

| Pillar | What it covers |
|---|---|
| A Thriving City Hall | Finding, navigating, and using government services |
| Thriving Neighborhoods | Development proposals, housing decisions, and the data behind them |
| Thriving Families | Youth and family services: jobs, education, public health |
| A Thriving Economy | Barriers small and minority-owned businesses hit in contracting |
| Thriving and Inclusive Communities | Finding trusted services without repeating your story to every agency |
| A Thriving and Sustainable Built Environment | Making the physical city legible: infrastructure, transit, street services |
| A City That Tells Its Stories | Surfacing cultural life and preserving community voices |

Read this for width, not for wording. Copying these is the failure mode: a pillar
that maps to nothing in your own anchor has no ally and no data, and the naming
pattern is one institution's house style rather than a template.
