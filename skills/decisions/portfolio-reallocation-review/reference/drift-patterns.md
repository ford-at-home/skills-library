# Drift patterns

Failure modes of the **portfolio**, not of the review. Naming one without citing the
initiative and the evidence is drift-flag theater — cite or drop it. Patterns
compound; one initiative can match several.

| Pattern | What it looks like | The check that exposes it |
|---|---|---|
| **Halo allocation** | Adjacent initiatives justified by a successful flagship's standing — "these are all part of the same theme" — with their own evidence thin or stale | Rank strictly per initiative. The flagship survives; do the others? |
| **Senior-sponsor protection** | The most senior person's initiative gets a softer case, a more forgiving assessment, and never appears on the considered-for-stopping list | The masked ranking in step 3. If the originator is also the only reviewer, get an independent one |
| **Kill-list theater** | Wind-down recommendations exist and are uniformly low-allocation, low-profile, or recently started | Force the weakest-case initiatives onto the considered list regardless of profile. A high-profile initiative with weaker evidence than one recommended for stopping is the finding |
| **Scope creep as amplification** | Allocation grew across reviews with no decision to grow it | Compare current allocation against the most recent *explicit* reallocation decision. The delta needs a retroactive amplify with cited evidence, or a return to the prior level |
| **Sunk-cost drift** | High cumulative allocation and age, with a rationale that references prior investment rather than expected return | Strip the history. Proposed today as a new candidate carrying only its forward-looking case, would you fund it? |
| **Diversification theater** | Many initiatives each too small to validate or fail cleanly, described as diversification | For each, what would it cost to actually validate within one period? If none is affordable, the portfolio is too wide |
| **Quiet candidate parking** | Candidates parked in this review that were parked in the last one, same reason unchanged | Force a decision on every parked candidate. A permanently parked candidate is a decision nobody has taken responsibility for |

A pattern recurring across several consecutive reviews is evidence that the
portfolio's governance is failing, not only its allocation. Escalate the recurrence,
not just the instance.
