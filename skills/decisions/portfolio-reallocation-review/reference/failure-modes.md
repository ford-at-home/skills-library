# How this goes wrong

**The one it exists to catch — portfolio drift.** The portfolio you have is the
residue of accumulated past decisions, not one anyone would author today. The
surface signal looks healthy: stable composition, a defensible story for each
initiative, total spend consistent with last period, nothing stopped in six months.
Underneath, the marginal allocation is incoherent — initiatives that should stop
continue because stopping is socially expensive, ones that should grow don't because
reallocation is operationally expensive, and candidates sit parked because deciding
either way costs political capital. The zero-based check is the only thing that
detects it, because every per-initiative review returns a defensible answer.

**Status-quo anchoring** is how the check itself fails: build the hypothetical after
inspecting the actual allocation and the two come out similar, so drift is invisible.
It is the rule most often broken by accident rather than on purpose.

Zero-based budgeting and cross-initiative comparison are decades-old practice and
nothing about them is novel here. What this contributes is the blind ordering, the
masked ranking, and the named drift patterns.
