# Bias patterns worth flagging

Only these, only with a quote. Each is one a friendly reading of the transcripts
will miss because the panel looks cooperative.

| Pattern | Look in | What it does to the conclusions |
|---|---|---|
| **Friendly panel** — respondents were pre-existing contacts | `selection_method`: "my network", "warm intro", "introduced by" | Stated preference is close to worthless; even behavioral claims may be performative |
| **Recruited via the problem** — respondents were told the problem first | `selection_method`: "people who struggle with…" | Confirms the problem by construction. The panel cannot disconfirm that it is widespread |
| **Single-segment collapse** — all respondents share an uncontrolled trait | `selection_method`, or the distribution | Conclusions generalize past the sample. Name the dimension; refuse validity outside it |
| **Solution-first framing** — the solution was described before the problem was asked about | ordering within transcripts | Respondents reverse-engineer a problem that fits. Reads as unusually well-aligned |
| **Hypothesis pre-loading** — the team's hypothesis appears in the script | `hypothesis_going_in` against `interview_method` | The interview was a search, not a test. All positive signal becomes `[assumption]` |
| **Missing disconfirmation** — `evidence_against` is empty | your own analysis | Either nobody disagreed, which is suspicious in a panel at or above the sample floor, or disagreement was filtered between the room and the notes. The second is more common |
| **Ignored dissenters** — a substantive disagreement was folded into a majority theme | what happened to the conflicts | Dissent in a small panel signals segment heterogeneity, usually the most decision-relevant thing in the data |

Patterns compound rather than average. A friendly panel asked leading questions
about features has produced no usable evidence, and the packet should say that.
