# How this goes wrong

Every one of these produces a synthesis that reads as evidence. That is why the
corresponding rules in SKILL.md are rules and not preferences — a packet built on
five interviews, uncited flags, or an interviewer's summary looks exactly like one
built properly.

**The one it exists to catch — prior laundering.** The team's hypothesis comes
back as customer signal because the panel was recruited, framed, or questioned in
a way that made agreement close to inevitable. The themes match the hypothesis and
everyone reads that as validation. Nothing is being hidden; the methodology
pre-loaded the answer and the interviews never had the ability to disconfirm
anything. Two checks catch it: `evidence_against` must be non-empty with its
emptiness flagged when it isn't, and the selection and question methods must be
audited with citations.

**Bias-flag theater.** Plausible flags with no citations, generated because the
section expected content. The citation gate is the only thing standing between
this skill and that output.

**Note-as-transcript.** An interviewer's filtered summary grounding a behavioral
claim. The interviewer already decided what mattered, and that decision is now
invisible and load-bearing.

**Enthusiasm as evidence.** "This is amazing, you should build this" recorded as
demand. Enthusiasm in an interview is courtesy; the behavioral question is what
they did afterwards, and it is almost never measured.
