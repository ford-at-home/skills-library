# Five ways a clearance passes a claim it should have caught

Each of these produces a fact base that reads as finished. That is why the
corresponding rules in SKILL.md are rules rather than preferences — nothing in
the output signals that one was violated.

**The one it exists to catch.** A claim appears in three internal documents and
the oldest cites no source. It reads as well-established *because* it's been
repeated. Trace it outside your organization. Two things reliably produce this:
a plan that was discussed and never executed, and an aspiration written in the
past tense.

**Vintage drift.** A claim cleared 18 months ago is still marked cleared. The
clearance was right; the world moved.

**Derived-as-measured.** A reconstructed figure sits in a table beside measured
ones at equal confidence.

**Clearing the easy ones.** Working in document order means the hard claims get
checked last, or never when the deadline lands.

**Hedge laundering.** A Block 1 claim comes back wrapped in "reportedly" and
passes on second read.
