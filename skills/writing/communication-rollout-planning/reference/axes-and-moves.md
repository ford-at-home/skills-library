# The three axes, and the move library

## The three axes

Which move to use depends on these more than on anything else.

**Size band.** As audience grows, visibility becomes permanent, accountability
diffuses, and register must tighten.

| Band | Size | Dynamic |
|---|---|---|
| `individual` | 1 | Full context, direct accountability |
| `micro` | 2–3 | Conversational; excluding a fourth carries meaning |
| `huddle` | 4–10 | Everyone knows everyone, still accountable |
| `team` | 11–30 | Bystander diffusion begins |
| `cross-team` | 31–100 | Strangers present; performative pressure |
| `org` | 101–500 | Public record. Expect forwarding and screenshots |
| `broadcast` | 500+ | Assume review by legal, HR, or press later |

**Composition.** A message to three people out of a ten-person group is not a
small-group message. It is an exclusion event, and both the three and the seven
read meaning into the slice.

`full-group` · `subset-of-known-group` · `assembled-for-purpose` · `individual`

**Valence.** The same words land differently depending on where the relationship
starts. This decides how much pre-negotiation a segment needs.

`ally` (aligned, low friction) · `neutral` (default) · `skeptic` (raises
concerns, not hostile) · `adversary` (opposing interests, no benefit of the
doubt)

## The moves

| Move | Good for | Reach | Avoid when |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1:1 sensitive conversation** | Content that personally affects the person: role change, comp, reorg impact, separation | 1 | It is routine; inflating it is its own signal |
| **Heads-up DM** | Stakeholders who will be asked about this, and anyone who would resent surprise | 1, repeated | The content is mundane, or the list is too long to preview |
| **Doc for comments** | Proposals, decisions, anything needing reviewable detail | high | Time-sensitive, or nobody will read it |
| **Async recorded walkthrough** | Visual or technical content, distributed timezones, will be re-watched | high | Purely textual, or captions matter and are not done |
| **FAQ document** | The same questions arriving repeatedly | high | Questions are too varied to consolidate |
| **Chat channel update** | Timely awareness to a defined audience, under 200 words | high | The audience does not live in that channel |
| **Chat announcement** (restricted) | Formal org-wide notice that must be findable later | high | Content still in flux |
| **Structured email** | External parties, formal notice, anything that must survive outside chat search | med–high | The audience lives in chat |
| **Meeting mention** (3 min) | Awareness plus signalling that the topic matters | high | The meeting is overloaded, or it needs more than five minutes |
| **Meeting demo** (10–15 min) | Showing beats telling, and live questions matter | high | The concept is abstract, better read |
| **Dedicated meeting** (30–60 min) | A decision needing live back-and-forth | med | The decision is already made |
| **Office hours** | Rollouts with unpredictable questions | med | Attendance will be sparse, or all questions are the same |
| **Availability poll** | Scheduling across calendars without thrash | high | Calendars are visible; just schedule it |
| **Feedback channel** | Long-tail questions in one searchable place | high | Volume will be low |
| **Follow-up meeting** | Committing to a convergence point before attention disperses | med | No decision is pending |
| **Recap post** | Consolidating decisions and owners after a sync event | high | The meeting was routine |

Rough ordering by cost: DMs and async artifacts are cheap for everyone;
broadcasts are cheap to send and expensive to correct; meetings are the most
expensive thing in the plan and should be the last resort, not the first move.
