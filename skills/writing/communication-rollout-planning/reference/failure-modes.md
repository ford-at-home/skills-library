# How this goes wrong

Every one of these produces a well-formed plan. That is why the corresponding
rules in SKILL.md are rules and not preferences — the defect is invisible in the
artifact and surfaces only once the plan has been executed.

**The one it exists to catch.** The plan starts with the broadcast because the
broadcast is the thing the sender was thinking about. Everyone who needed a
warning gets one afterwards, framed as a courtesy, and reads it as damage
control.

**Exclusion leakage.** A message goes to four of nine people with no explanation
of why those four. The other five find out — they always do — and the subject of
the message becomes the slice, not the content.

**Padding.** Every available move gets included because the library is right
there. Nine moves for a routine change, and the sender executes three of them,
picking by convenience rather than by the ordering rules.

**Invented specifics.** The draft copy names a date, a channel, or a metric the
sender never supplied. Because everything around it is right, it ships.

**A stale profile.** Channels renamed, people moved, cadences changed. The plan
is well-formed and routes through a channel that no longer exists. Re-read the
profile every run and treat anything the sender contradicts as stale.

**Compressed sensitivity under a deadline.** The 1:1s are dropped because the
broadcast date is fixed. The date was the only movable thing in the plan.
