# Piece architecture

Sentence-level voice does not build a piece. Four moves are the minimum for the
piece to be doing anything at all:

1. **Casual entry.** Open with a scene or a concrete event, not the claim. A
   reader who thinks they are hearing a story keeps reading; a reader who meets a
   thesis statement starts arguing.
2. **A named gap** between what everyone says about the subject and what
   everyone knows about it. If you cannot name the gap, this is a topic, not a
   piece — say so rather than drafting around the hole.
3. **An implicated narrator.** Where the piece criticizes a pattern, it names
   where the author does it too. Standing outside as judge turns the piece into
   propaganda.
4. **A reframing closer.** The last line changes what the piece was about. Not a
   summary, not a moral.

Two supporting rules. Every abstract claim gets a moment underneath it where a
specific person did a specific thing — abstractions do not argue, scenes do. And
where an image or phrase recurs, it should mean something heavier the second
time, not merely reappear.
