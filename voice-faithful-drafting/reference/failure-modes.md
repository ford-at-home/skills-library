# How this goes wrong

Every one of these produces a piece that reads well. That is why the
corresponding rules in SKILL.md are rules and not preferences — nothing
downstream signals that one was violated.

**The one it exists to catch.** Asked to write in someone's voice with no brief,
a model produces prose that is fluent, on-topic, structurally sound, and aimed at
nobody. It is hard to reject because there is nothing wrong with it. The persona
and throughline are the only inputs that make a sentence rejectable.

**The inferred brief.** Sources are read, a plausible persona and throughline are
extracted, and drafting proceeds. The output then agrees with the sources about
what matters, which is the one thing it was supposed to test.

**Style without anti-style.** Only the voice file is loaded. The signature moves
appear, the tells stay, and the result reads like an impression of the voice.

**Accent creep.** Three accent layers in one piece. Each is recognizable and the
piece sounds like nobody, because a voice that can do everything is not a voice.

**Silent scrubbing.** A strong claim is softened while removing a hedge stack.
The prose improves and the argument loses.

**Gap-filling under deadline.** `[Example needed: …]` becomes a plausible
invented anecdote. This is the single most expensive failure here, because
invented specifics are exactly what the voice is best at making convincing.

**Throughline drift in multi-channel runs.** The short channel gets a slightly
different argument because it had to fit. Two pieces now say two things.
